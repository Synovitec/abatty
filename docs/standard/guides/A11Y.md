# Accessibility: best practices

Operational reference guide. The Belgian and European legal frame, the six errors that actually matter, technical patterns, tooling and CI. Sources at the end. The examples use Next.js / React; the rules hold for any front end.

---

## 1. Legal frame (Belgium, 2026)

### Which standard

**WCAG 2.2 level AA** is the target. It has been the W3C recommendation since October 2023 and became ISO/IEC 40500:2025 in October 2025.

**WCAG 3.0 is not a constraint.** Still a Working Draft (latest version March 2026, 174 requirements), Candidate Recommendation expected around the end of 2027, final recommendation not before 2028 to 2030. No law references it. Building for 2.2 AA is already building the foundation 3.0 will reward.

**EN 301 549** is the European technical standard that gives a presumption of conformity. It aligns with WCAG. It is what audits in Europe cite, including for institutions (EEAS / Web Accessibility Directive context).

### Who is covered in Belgium

The European Accessibility Act (EU Directive 2019/882) has applied since **28 June 2025**. Belgium transposed it by amending existing laws rather than through a single act, the core being the Belgian Accessibility Act adopted in November 2023.

Digital services covered: **e-commerce**, consumer banking services, e-books, telecoms, audiovisual media, self-service terminals. The notion of an e-commerce service is broad: any service through a site or an application whose purpose is to conclude a contract with a consumer.

**Exemptions:**

- Micro-enterprises (fewer than 10 employees **and** turnover or balance sheet total under 2 million euros) are exempt from the service obligations. In Belgium, micro-enterprises doing e-commerce have until 2030.
- Content published before 28 June 2025, or coming from a third party outside your control (pre-recorded media, office files, archives, online maps).
- Disproportionate burden, but it must be assessed and justified in writing, not merely invoked.
- Fundamental alteration of the service.

Extraterritorial reach: a company outside the EU that sells to European consumers is covered.

### Sanctions and remedies

Complaints can be filed directly with the company, through a public authority, or by organisations acting on behalf of the people concerned. The first EAA court actions were filed in France in November 2025. Fine amounts vary widely between member states, from about 60,000 euros in Ireland to about 900,000 euros in Sweden. Authorities can also impose an audit, ban a product from the national market, or publish the non-conformity.

### What it means commercially

For an agency, a11y is no longer an optional selling point. It is a contractual requirement on any e-commerce project for a client above the micro-enterprise threshold. Put it in the standard quote: building accessible from the start has very little impact on the total budget, unlike a remediation afterwards.

## 2. The six errors that make up 96 % of the problem

The WebAIM Million 2026 scanned the million most visited home pages. **95.9 % had automatically detectable WCAG 2 failures**, up from 94.8 % in 2025, reversing six years of gradual improvement. An average of 56.1 errors per page, up 10.1 %.

| Failure type                       | % of home pages |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Low-contrast text                  | 83.9 %          |
| Missing alternative text on images | 53.1 %          |
| Missing form labels                | 51 %            |
| Empty links                        | 46.3 %          |
| Empty buttons                      | 30.6 %          |
| Missing document language          | 13.5 %          |

These six categories account for 96 % of all detected errors, and they have been the same for seven years. None is an exotic edge case. They are the basics.

Fixing only these six points puts a site above 95 % of the web.

### Why it is regressing

Page complexity is exploding: 1,437 elements per home page on average, up 22.5 % in a year, nearly doubling in seven years. WebAIM explicitly attributes the trend to growing reliance on third-party frameworks and assisted coding practices, "vibe coding".

The point for your own code: the more complex the component, the more it breaks. Simplifying is an accessibility strategy.

## 3. Semantic HTML first

It is 80 % of the work and it is free.

```html
<header>
  <nav>
    <main>
      <article>
        <section>
          <aside>
            <footer>
              <button>
                <a href>
                  <label>
                    <fieldset>
                      <legend>
                        <table>
                          <th scope></th>
                        </table>
                      </legend></fieldset></label
                ></a>
              </button>
            </footer>
          </aside>
        </section>
      </article>
    </main>
  </nav>
</header>
```

A `<div onClick>` is not a button. It has no role, no focus, no keyboard handling, no pressed state. A `<button>` has all of that for free.

### Non-negotiable rules

- `<html lang="fr">` on every page, updated when the locale changes. It is one line and 13.5 % of sites miss it.
- One `<h1>` per page. 18.1 % of pages have several.
- No skipped heading level. 41.8 % of pages skip levels. Headings are the main navigation mechanism for screen-reader users.
- One `<main>` per page. Only 46.1 % of pages have one.
- A skip link to the main content. Only 17.1 % of pages have one, and one in ten is broken (either hidden inaccessibly or pointing at a missing target).

```html
<a href="#main" class="skip-link">Skip to main content</a>
...
<main id="main" tabindex="-1"></main>
```

```css
.skip-link {
  position: absolute;
  left: -9999px;
}
.skip-link:focus {
  left: 1rem;
  top: 1rem;
  z-index: 100;
}
```

Never use `display: none` to hide a skip link; it then becomes unreachable by keyboard.

## 4. ARIA: the five rules

Raw data from the WebAIM Million 2026: pages with ARIA had **59.1 errors on average against 42 for pages without ARIA**. The number of ARIA attributes rose 27 % in a year, to 133 per page on average, six times more than in 2019. The more ARIA there is, the more errors there are.

Hence the established formula: **no ARIA is better than bad ARIA**.

The five W3C rules:

1. **Do not use ARIA if a native HTML element does the job.** `<button>`, `<a href>`, `<input type="checkbox">`, `<details>` bring role, focus and keyboard for free.
2. **Do not override native semantics.** Never `<h1 role="button">` or `<div role="checkbox">` when `<input type="checkbox">` exists.
3. **Every interactive control must be usable by keyboard.** What can be done with a mouse must be doable with a keyboard.
4. **Do not put `role="presentation"` or `aria-hidden="true"` on a focusable element.** It becomes an invisible keyboard trap.
5. **Every interactive element must have an accessible name.**

A concrete alarm signal: 5.7 % of pages have a `role="menu"`, and 22 % of those ARIA menus create barriers for lack of the required markup and interactions. If an ARIA pattern is hard to implement correctly, the component is probably too complicated and should be simplified.

Per-page averages to watch in your own code: 23.3 `aria-hidden="true"`, 30.4 `tabindex` per page in the 2026 sample. A positive `tabindex` is almost always a bug.

## 5. Contrast and colour

The web's first problem, on 83.9 % of pages, with 34 distinct occurrences per page on average.

### WCAG 2.2 AA thresholds

| Element                                                 | Minimum ratio                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Normal text                                             | 4.5:1                               |
| Large text (24px, or 18.66px bold)                      | 3:1                                 |
| UI components and information-bearing graphics (1.4.11) | 3:1                                 |
| Focus indicator                                         | 3:1 against the adjacent background |

### Frequent traps

- Light grey text on a white background in placeholders and legal notices.
- Text over an image or a gradient, where the ratio varies by area.
- Dark mode added afterwards: colour tokens must be checked in both themes, not only in light.
- Hover and disabled states, often forgotten in audits.
- Colour alone as the carrier of information (1.4.1). A field in error needs a text message and an icon, not only a red border.

Check the design-system tokens once, in both themes, rather than component by component. The standard asks for that computation to be a script over the token file (A11Y-1), not a reading by eye.

## 6. Keyboard and focus

The most cost-effective test there is: walk an entire page with the Tab key.

- Everything clickable must be reachable by keyboard, in a logical order that follows the visual order.
- The focus indicator must stay visible. Never `outline: none` without a replacement.
- **WCAG 2.2, 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA)**: the element receiving focus must not be entirely hidden by author-created content. In practice: sticky headers, cookie banners, floating chats. It is the new criterion modern sites fail most.
- Focus trap in modals: focus must enter the modal, stay inside while it is open, and return to the triggering element on close. Escape must close.

```css
:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid var(--focus-ring);
  outline-offset: 2px;
}
```

`:focus-visible` avoids showing the ring on a mouse click while keeping it for the keyboard.

## 7. What is new in WCAG 2.2

Nine criteria added, and 4.1.1 Parsing removed as obsolete (browsers fix markup errors consistently, and the real problems remain covered by 1.3.1 and 4.1.2).

**Level A**

- **3.2.6 Consistent Help**: help mechanisms repeated across pages must appear in the same relative order.
- **3.3.7 Redundant Entry**: information already entered in the same process must be pre-filled or offered for selection, not re-entered.

**Level AA**

- **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)**: see section 6.
- **2.5.7 Dragging Movements**: any action requiring a drag must have a single-click alternative. Concerns sliders, carousels, reordering, maps.
- **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)**: targets of 24 x 24 CSS pixels minimum, or equivalent spacing. The only one of the nine that can be tested automatically. The house rule keeps 44 px on mobile surfaces.
- **3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum)**: no mandatory cognitive function test in authentication. Concretely: allow password paste, support password managers, do not impose a puzzle or a character transcription without an alternative. A TOTP field accepts paste.

**Level AAA** (2.4.12, 2.4.13, 3.3.9): optional, useful as a quality target on a polished project.

Eight of the nine need manual verification.

## 8. Forms

51 % of pages miss labels, and a third of form fields are not correctly labelled (neither via `<label>`, nor `aria-label`, nor `aria-labelledby`, nor `title`).

```html
<label for="email">Email address</label>
<input
  id="email"
  name="email"
  type="email"
  autocomplete="email"
  aria-describedby="email-hint email-error"
  aria-invalid="true"
/>
<p id="email-hint">We never share your address.</p>
<p id="email-error" role="alert">Invalid address format.</p>
```

Key points:

- A placeholder is not a label. It disappears on input.
- `autocomplete` with the standard values (1.3.5): a real gain for users with motor or cognitive impairments, and a conversion bonus.
- Errors must be announced: `role="alert"` or a live region, and a link from an error summary at the top of the form to each faulty field.
- Group radios and checkboxes in `<fieldset>` with `<legend>`.
- Do not validate only on a silent blur. The screen-reader user does not see the red message appear.

## 9. Images, links, buttons

**Images**: 16.2 % of images have no alternative text, and 10.8 % of those that have one have a dubious or repetitive alt ("image", "graphic", a file name, or text identical to the neighbouring text). In total, more than one image in four is a problem.

- Decorative image: `alt=""`, never a missing alt attribute.
- Informative image: describe the information, not the image.
- Image inside a link: the alt becomes the link text. 45 % of images without alt are linked images, which produces non-descriptive links. One linked image in four has no alt.

**Empty links and empty buttons** (46.3 % and 30.6 % of pages): almost always icons without text.

```html
<button aria-label="Close the window">
  <svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false">...</svg>
</button>
```

**Ambiguous link text**: 15.2 % of pages have "click here", "learn more", "continue" links. A screen reader can list links out of context; they become unusable.

## 10. Animation and motion

Relevant for an animation-oriented stack (reveal on scroll, animated components).

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *,
  *::before,
  *::after {
    animation-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    transition-duration: 0.01ms !important;
    scroll-behavior: auto !important;
  }
}
```

Associated rules:

- **2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide**: any moving, blinking or auto-scrolling content lasting more than 5 seconds must be controllable. Autoplay carousels are concerned.
- **2.3.1 Three Flashes**: nothing that flashes more than three times per second.
- Scroll-reveal animations must never leave content invisible if JavaScript fails or if the user has enabled reduced motion. Content must be visible by default with the animation added on top, not the reverse.

```tsx
const prefersReducedMotion = useReducedMotion();

<motion.div
  initial={prefersReducedMotion ? false : { opacity: 0, y: 20 }}
  whileInView={{ opacity: 1, y: 0 }}
  viewport={{ once: true }}
/>;
```

## 11. React / Next.js specifics

Measured good news: Next.js pages had 40.9 errors on average against 56.1 for the overall sample, 27.1 % fewer. React is at 43.5. Server rendering and structured components really help.

### Points specific to a SPA / App Router

- **Route change**: on client-side navigation, focus does not return to the start of the document and the page change is not announced. Move focus to the `<h1>` or the `<main>` on route change, and announce the page title via a live region.
- **Page title**: `generateMetadata` per route, unique and descriptive titles (2.4.2).
- **`lang` and `dir`**: `<html lang={locale} dir={dir}>`. With i18n, compute `dir` for Arabic and Hebrew.
- **Modals and popovers**: prefer the native `<dialog>` or a tested primitive (Radix UI, React Aria) over a home-made implementation. The focus trap, focus restoration and `aria-modal` are hard to write correctly.
- **Third-party libraries**: the WebAIM data shows that nearly every popular JS library (carousels, lightboxes, custom selects) correlates with a rise in errors. Swiper, Slick, OWL Carousel, Select2, FancyBox are all above average. Audit every UI dependency before adopting it.
- **E-commerce**: e-commerce platforms are the worst of the panel (Shopify +33.9 %, Magento +35 %, PrestaShop +155.3 %). A well-made custom checkout beats the default theme.
- **Live regions** for dynamic changes (add to cart, search result, toast).

```tsx
<div role="status" aria-live="polite" aria-atomic="true">
  {message}
</div>
```

`aria-live="polite"` for information, `assertive` only for the urgent. The container must exist in the DOM before the message is injected, otherwise nothing is announced.

### Component libraries and lint

`eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y` only sees a library's markup (MUI, shadcn) if `settings['jsx-a11y'].components` maps each primitive to its native element AND `polymorphicPropName` is set. Without both, it reports zero on code that fails. The standard holds this as a tooling rule (A11Y-1).

## 12. Tooling and CI

### What automation catches

A Deque study of more than 2,000 audits, 13,000 pages and nearly 300,000 issues shows that automated testing via axe-core covers on average **57 % of the total volume of issues**, more than the common belief of 20 to 30 %. But 57 % is not 100 %: keyboard, context, alt relevance and journey logic remain manual.

Conversely, WebAIM reminds us that the absence of detected errors does not mean a page is accessible or compliant.

### Recommended chain

**1. Lint while writing**

```bash
npm i -D eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y
```

Catches the obvious faults in the editor: missing alt, handler on a non-interactive element, positive `tabindex`, anchor without href.

**2. Automated tests in CI**

```bash
npm i -D @axe-core/playwright
```

```ts
import AxeBuilder from "@axe-core/playwright";

test("home page has no a11y violation", async ({ page }) => {
  await page.goto("/fr");
  const results = await new AxeBuilder({ page })
    .withTags(["wcag2a", "wcag2aa", "wcag21aa", "wcag22aa"])
    .analyze();

  expect(results.violations).toEqual([]);
});
```

Fail CI on violations. Cover at minimum: home, product page, checkout, contact form, login page, and the state after a modal opens.

**3. Manual verification before going live**

A short checklist, a 20-minute pass:

- Full keyboard walk, focus always visible and never trapped
- Browser zoom at 200 % then 400 %, nothing breaks, no horizontal scroll
- A screen reader on the critical journey (VoiceOver on macOS, NVDA on Windows, both free)
- Token contrast in both themes
- A test with CSS disabled to check the logical content order

**4. One-off tools**

- WAVE (browser extension), the engine used by the WebAIM report
- axe DevTools (extension)
- Lighthouse, accessibility section, useful but shallow
- WebAIM Contrast Checker for tokens

### What not to do

Accessibility overlays (JavaScript widgets promising compliance in one line of script) do not make a site compliant and are widely rejected by the community and by the people concerned. They give no legal protection.

## 13. Accessibility statement

The Belgian FPS Economy translates the EAA obligations for e-commerce and banking services into concrete points, including: provide information through at least two different sensory channels, guarantee readability by screen readers, describe non-text content, allow font adaptation, and **inform about the service's accessibility and its compatibility with assistive technologies**.

That last point implies a public, up-to-date accessibility statement page stating:

- The targeted conformance level (WCAG 2.2 AA) and the actual state
- The known non-conforming content and why
- Where applicable, the disproportionate burden invoked and its assessment
- A contact to report a problem, and the available remedy

A missing or incomplete statement is one of the most frequently recorded grounds of non-conformity.

## 14. Project checklist

**Design**

- [ ] Contrast validated on every token, in light and in dark
- [ ] Focus indicator defined in the design system
- [ ] Touch targets 24 px minimum, 44 px on mobile
- [ ] No information carried by colour alone
- [ ] Mock-ups planned for 200 % zoom

**Development**

- [ ] `lang` and `dir` correct, updated on locale change
- [ ] One `<h1>` per page, hierarchy without skips
- [ ] `<main>`, `<nav>`, `<header>`, `<footer>` present
- [ ] Working skip link
- [ ] Every field labelled, `autocomplete` filled in
- [ ] Every icon button has an accessible name
- [ ] Alt on every image, `alt=""` on decorative ones
- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` respected
- [ ] Focus managed on route change
- [ ] Modals: focus trap, Escape, focus restoration
- [ ] No positive `tabindex`

**CI**

- [ ] `eslint-plugin-jsx-a11y` on, at error, with the component mapping
- [ ] axe in automated tests on the critical journeys, blocking
- [ ] Token contrast computed by a script, in both themes
- [ ] Manual keyboard pass before every production release

**Delivery**

- [ ] Accessibility statement published
- [ ] Reporting channel in place
- [ ] Disproportionate-burden assessment documented if invoked
